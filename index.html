Carnot 2026 — Photonic Heat Engines

A two-day workshop on optical refrigeration, radiation-balanced lasers, and high-power laser source development.

A laser run backwards is a refrigerator. Everything interesting happens in between.

Dates24–25 October 2026
VenuePAIS, University of New Mexico
CityAlbuquerque, New Mexico
FormatTwo days, single track, ~30 people

The premise

Entropy is a design variable, not a bookkeeping detail.

Photons carry entropy, and once you take that seriously a laser stops being a light source and becomes a heat engine. Run it forward and it must reject heat. Run it in reverse and it cools its own crystal — optical refrigeration, cooling a solid by anti-Stokes fluorescence. Run it exactly at the stall point, where gain and cooling balance, and you have a radiation-balanced laser that produces no net heat at all.

Attending to those cycles and their fluctuations is what lets designers push average power without paying for it in linewidth and frequency stability. It is the regime where high-power source design and thermal management stop being separable engineering problems — and it is what this workshop is about.

The Carnot workshop continues the Photonic Heat Engines community, which met annually for eight editions within a larger photonics meeting through 2024. This is its first independently organized meeting.

Session themes

I · Optical refrigeration — rare earthsYb-doped fluorides, ultrapure crystal growth, and how far below ambient a solid can be driven.
II · Optical refrigeration — semiconductorsBulk crystals and nanostructures, luminescence quantum yield, and the barriers to net cooling.
III · Radiation-balanced lasersThe stall point, where gain and anti-Stokes cooling cancel and the crystal carries no net heat load.
IV · High-power sources and applicationsThermal management at power, linewidth and frequency stability, and hardware that has to leave the bench. Closes with the round-table.
V · New workFlash talks and the poster session — students and postdocs presenting results that are still in motion.

Poster session, with prizes

Around twelve posters are expected, drawn from the speakers’ groups, from UNM’s Center for High Technology Materials, and from the national laboratories. The session has its own ninety-minute block on Day One with a reception, introduced by flash talks in which every presenter gets two minutes and one slide.

Poster prizes are awarded at the workshop dinner, and the winners are invited to give short talks in Sunday’s contributed session.

Venue and travel

Albuquerque, late October

The workshop meets in the Physics, Astronomy and Interdisciplinary Sciences building on the University of New Mexico main campus. Los Alamos, Sandia, the Air Force Research Laboratory and UNM's Center for High Technology Materials are all within reach of the same room — which is why the field's center of gravity has moved here.

Late October is the high desert at its best: hard blue skies, dry air, cool mornings, and balloons still going up at dawn.
